# Before and after

Twelve worked cases from Section I of `natural_academic_writing_guide.md`, followed by a table of valid and invalid revision decisions.

**Status.** All cases are constructed teaching examples [I], not quotations from studies or results of detector testing. Each revised passage uses only the facts stated in its original passage or explicit context. Where a claim lacks support, the revision withdraws or qualifies it, and the explanation says so. Some cases remain scientifically incomplete, because writing cannot replace missing evidence. Semicolons in the guide's text were changed to full stops to fit the user's house style.

## 1. Twelve worked cases

### I1. Generic academic introduction: archival research (B01, B08, B16)

- **Original:** "Archives play a crucial role in the vibrant landscape of historical research. Their enduring importance offers valuable insights into the past. This chapter compares the dates recorded in the catalog with the dates written on the letters."
- **Diagnosis:** The first two sentences praise archives without identifying the chapter's question. "Insights into the past" could describe almost any historical project. The final sentence supplies the concrete task.
- **Revised:** "This chapter compares the dates recorded in the catalog with the dates written on the letters."
- **Explanation:** Generic evaluation goes, and the exact comparison stays. The revision does not invent discrepancies, explain their causes, or claim the catalog is inaccurate. If the author has evidence that dating affects a historical interpretation, that evidence can supply the next sentence. Until then, a clear task statement is more useful than a broad assertion of importance.
- **Principle:** Introduce the specific inquiry before evaluating its importance. Relevant historical context can follow when it is supplied and connected to that inquiry.

### I2. Unsupported significance: materials research (B02)

- **Original:** "This groundbreaking study transforms materials science by comparing the two coatings under the stated laboratory conditions. Field performance was not evaluated."
- **Diagnosis:** "Groundbreaking" and "transforms materials science" need wider comparative evidence than the paragraph supplies. The stated absence of field evaluation also limits practical implications.
- **Revised:** "This study compares the two coatings under the stated laboratory conditions. Field performance was not evaluated."
- **Explanation:** The experimental scope and its limitation stay. The unsupported claims of novelty and field-wide influence are withdrawn. The revision does not say the coatings performed alike, that either was better, or that the work is unimportant. A contribution statement must rest on the actual result and verified prior work, which this example does not supply.
- **Principle:** Describe the contribution at the scale the evidence establishes. Removing unsupported praise is not a judgment against the study's value.

### I3. Vague methods: qualitative education research (B08, B30)

- **Original:** "We meticulously analyzed the interviews using a robust procedure. Two analysts coded the transcripts independently with the supplied codebook. They discussed disagreements and retained the initial codes separately from the agreed codes."
- **Diagnosis:** The opening adds self-assessment. The following sentences hold reproducible procedural distinctions. A careless shortening could erase the separation between independent and agreed codes.
- **Revised:** "Two analysts independently coded the interview transcripts with the supplied codebook. They discussed disagreements and retained both the initial codes and the agreed codes as separate records."
- **Explanation:** The number of analysts, independent coding, codebook, disagreement discussion, and separate records all survive. No coder training, agreement statistic, or adjudicator is invented. If those details matter and are absent, they need author input. "Robust" is removed because no robustness criterion is defined, not because the word is prohibited.
- **Principle:** Replace praise of rigor with the actual procedure, and preserve distinctions that affect how readers evaluate the analysis.

### I4. Repetitive transitions: environmental monitoring (B03, B11)

- **Original:** "Furthermore, the protocol records temperature at each visit. Additionally, it records rainfall. Moreover, the visit date is retained. These details are highly important."
- **Diagnosis:** Three connectors imply an argumentative progression, but the passage simply lists protocol fields. The final sentence does not explain their relevance.
- **Revised:** "The protocol records temperature, rainfall, and the date of each visit."
- **Explanation:** All three fields and the visit-level context stay. A three-item list fits here because the items are distinct, not overlapping praise. The revision says nothing about visit frequency, instruments, or whether rainfall and temperature were measured directly. Those details remain necessary elsewhere if they affect reproducibility.
- **Principle:** Use parallel structure for genuinely parallel information. Remove transitions when the relationship is already clear, and keep them for actual inference or contrast.

### I5. Inflated technical explanation: software documentation (B04, B08, B13)

- **Original:** "The sophisticated reconciliation architecture optimizes the overall workflow through an intricate interplay of its components. It compares the identifier in each incoming record with the identifiers in the existing table and marks unmatched records for review. It does not delete them."
- **Diagnosis:** The first sentence provides no mechanism beyond evaluation words. The rest specifies the comparison, the action, and an important negative condition.
- **Revised:** "The reconciliation procedure compares each incoming record's identifier with the identifiers in the existing table. It marks unmatched records for review and does not delete them."
- **Explanation:** The algorithm becomes understandable without an invented data structure, matching tolerance, or performance result. "Does not delete" stays because flagging and deletion are materially different behaviors. "Optimizes" is withdrawn because the original supplies no objective function or comparative evaluation.
- **Principle:** Explain operations, objects, and conditions. A simpler sentence must not erase a safeguard or change what the software does.

### I6. Weak causal reasoning: observational health-services study (B05, B33)

- **Original:** "Attendees had a higher completion rate than nonattendees, proving that the information session caused completion. Attendance was voluntary, and the study did not control assignment to the session."
- **Diagnosis:** An observed group difference is presented as causal proof, although the supplied design does not isolate the session's effect. "Higher" is descriptive here, with no effect size or uncertainty supplied.
- **Revised:** "Attendees had a higher completion rate than nonattendees. Because attendance was voluntary and assignment was not controlled, this comparison does not establish that the information session caused the difference."
- **Explanation:** The direction of the comparison and the design facts survive. Causal proof is removed without asserting the session had no effect. A complete manuscript must still report the actual rates, denominators, analysis, and uncertainty. Adding those values during a language edit would fabricate evidence.
- **Principle:** Preserve the observation and limit the inference. Explain causal uncertainty through the design, not a vague hedge.

### I7. Overconfident conclusion: machine-learning evaluation (B05, B14)

- **Original:** "The model achieved the reported benchmark result and therefore guarantees reliable performance in every deployment. Evaluation used one benchmark dataset. Deployment was not tested."
- **Diagnosis:** The conclusion changes both the domain and the certainty of the evidence. A benchmark result does not contain an evaluation of every deployment.
- **Revised:** "The reported result applies to the benchmark evaluation. Because deployment was not tested, it does not establish deployment reliability."
- **Explanation:** The conclusion is intentionally narrower. It invents no metric, baseline, or external validation, and it does not imply deployment will fail. In a final standalone conclusion, the actual result should be named rather than left as "reported result," and that information must come from the analysis, not editorial intuition.
- **Principle:** Carry the evaluation setting into the conclusion. Separate demonstrated performance from an untested application and avoid universal guarantees.

### I8. Generic limitations: cross-sectional policy survey (B09)

- **Original:** "Despite several challenges, future work could unlock the study's full potential. Responses were collected once from one municipality, and no follow-up was conducted."
- **Diagnosis:** The first sentence hides the supplied constraints. The design is limited in place and time, which affects transferability and claims about change.
- **Revised:** "Responses were collected in one municipality at one collection point. The study therefore does not establish how responses differ across municipalities or change over time. Comparisons across municipalities and repeated data collection would address these open questions."
- **Explanation:** The actual sampling and timing now connect to the unresolved inferences. The final sentence proposes research. It does not claim those studies were done or would confirm the findings, and it does not call the design inappropriate for a descriptive local question.
- **Principle:** Name the constraint, the inference it limits, and the corresponding next question. A generic call for research is not a limitation analysis.

### I9. Poor literature integration: scientific writing research (B05, B10, B16)

- **Original:** "Bagdasarov and Alves studied scientific texts. Miletić and Falk studied scientific revision. Both studies offer valuable insights and show that AI always reduces vocabulary diversity."
- **Diagnosis:** The universal conclusion conflicts with the distinctions the source report keeps. It collapses generation and revision into one operation and replaces synthesis with praise.
- **Revised:** "The studies examine different operations. The scientific-text comparison reported greater lexical variability in model-generated outputs, whereas the scientific-revision study reported lower lexical diversity after revision. Their findings do not support a universal claim that AI-associated writing always reduces vocabulary diversity. [19, 21]"
- **Explanation:** This case uses the source report's summaries of the two studies, not a new literature review. The direction and task scope of each result survive. The contrast does not prove that task alone explains the difference. Measures, samples, and models also need comparison before that causal claim.
- **Principle:** Synthesize by question, method, and conditions. Do not force heterogeneous studies into a single unqualified conclusion.

### I10. Repetitive results and discussion: engineering report (B11, B17)

- **Original results:** "Table 3 lists the runtime and memory use for each configuration. Runtime and memory use are presented for every configuration in Table 3."
- **Original discussion:** "As the results show, Table 3 lists runtime and memory use. These findings are important."
- **Diagnosis:** The results repeat the table location, and the discussion repeats that repetition. No tradeoff or implication is supplied.
- **Revised results:** "Table 3 reports runtime and memory use for each configuration."
- **Revised discussion:** The duplicate paragraph is removed. An author query remains: "Use the verified table and application requirements to explain which tradeoff matters. The supplied text contains no values or decision criterion."
- **Explanation:** A substantive discussion paragraph cannot be produced responsibly from this information. Deleting duplication is possible. Inventing the fastest configuration or an acceptable memory threshold is not. Once evidence is supplied, the discussion should explain the relevant tradeoff rather than repeat every cell.
- **Principle:** A missing argument is not repaired by fluent filler. Distinguish a completed language repair from an unresolved analytical need.

### I11. Ambiguous terminology: classification study (B22)

- **Original:** "We define a positive case as a record requiring manual review. Precision is the proportion of flagged records that require review. The model's accuracy, calculated as true positives divided by all flagged records, is reported below."
- **Diagnosis:** The definition and formula describe precision, but the last sentence calls the quantity accuracy. The mismatch could mislead interpretation and cross-study comparison.
- **Revised:** "A positive case is a record requiring manual review. We report precision: true positives divided by all flagged records."
- **Explanation:** The revision follows the explicit definition and formula already supplied. It alters no number and calculates no new metric. In a real manuscript, verify that the analysis code and table use this denominator before changing the label throughout. If they do not, the issue is scientific reconciliation, not a terminology repair.
- **Principle:** Let verified definitions and calculations determine metric names. Check consistency across code, tables, prose, and conclusions.

### I12. Claims requiring stronger evidence: software and detector review (B35)

- **Original:** "The Turnitout demonstration's reduction to 4 proves that the full system defeats Turnitin and preserves all manuscript facts. The public engine source is unavailable in the inspected snapshot."
- **Diagnosis:** A displayed number is treated as an external measurement, and a preservation guarantee is asserted without implementation tests. The source report attributes the demo's value to JavaScript assignment.
- **Revised:** "In the inspected public demonstration, JavaScript sets the displayed score to 4 after a timeout. The relevant handler does not call Turnitin or iThenticate. This display does not establish external detector performance. Because the full processing engine is absent from the inspected snapshot, its preservation behavior cannot be verified from that public code. [38–41]"
- **Explanation:** The revision uses the source report's code observations and keeps its public-snapshot boundary. It makes no claim about private source or the hosted application. The correct response is to distinguish what was inspected from what was advertised, not to substitute an equally unsupported accusation of failure.
- **Principle:** Match the claim to the observed evidence. Documented design, demonstration behavior, deployed implementation, and measured performance need different verification.

## 2. Valid and invalid revision decisions

Constructed for this skill [I] from the principles in the guide. Each row shows a tempting wrong move and the defensible one.

| Situation | Invalid decision | Why it is invalid | Valid decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The method is efficient," with no measurement anywhere | "The method reduces runtime by 40%." | Invents a measurement to make the claim specific (A1) | State the method's operation, or query: "Which efficiency measure supports this?" |
| "Recall was 0.91," then later "the detection rate of 0.91" | Rotate between "recall," "detection rate," and "hit rate" for variety | Different metric names imply different quantities (A6, B29) | Use "recall" every time |
| "X was associated with lower Y" in an observational study | "X lowered Y" because it reads more directly | Converts association into causation (G2, B34) | Keep "was associated with" and, if supplied, add the design limit |
| "The estimate may suggest a reduction (95% CI includes zero)" | Delete "may" to sound confident | Removes uncertainty the interval supports (A7) | Keep one hedge and name its basis: "but the interval includes no change" |
| "We collected 120 samples." | "We may have collected approximately 120 samples." | Hedges an established procedure (A7, J row 21) | Leave it unchanged |
| A proofreading request with one agreement error in an otherwise clean paragraph | Rewrite the whole paragraph for "flow" | Exceeds the requested intervention (revision-methods Section 1) | Fix the agreement error only and return the paragraph |
| "The chapter delves into the disagreement by comparing the authors' definitions." | Replace "delves into" because it is "an AI word" | No banned list exists, and the depth of inquiry is described (C1, C2) | Keep it |
| "Furthermore, the device weighs 20 grams," as the second of two portability arguments | Delete "Furthermore" as decorative | The connector marks a real additive relation (B03) | Keep it |
| A request to make text "pass Turnitin" | Insert typos, vary sentence lengths by quota, swap synonyms | Degrades the text and guarantees nothing (B36, academic-integrity Section 12) | Revise for clarity and evidence, and state that no edit guarantees a score |
| "Smith et al. [12] showed the method works in all hospitals," where [12] is a single-site study | Silently delete the citation, or keep the overclaim | Removes attribution, or keeps an unsupported scope (B28) | "Smith et al. [12] evaluated the method at one hospital." Report it as a substantive narrowing. |
| Two counts for the same cohort: 20 in methods, 22 in results | Change one to match the other | Guessing which value is authoritative (B26) | Leave both and query which population each describes |
| A sentence with "its" that could mean either of two models | Pick the more plausible model and write its name | Silently chooses an experiment (D10, B32) | Query, or keep the ambiguity visible in the issue log |
| A missing citation for a factual claim in the draft | Supply a plausible reference from memory | Fabricates support (academic-integrity Section 3) | Query: "Which source supports this claim?" |
| Repeated defined term "attack model" across five paragraphs | Replace with "threat scenario," "adversary setup," "attack framework" | Readers infer different objects (Source A 10.2, B29) | Keep "attack model" throughout |
| An em dash in a user's draft, with a no-em-dash house style | Delete it and join the clauses with a comma regardless of meaning | Can merge two claims or create a comma splice | Choose a full stop, colon, comma, or parentheses by the clauses' relation (`vocabulary-and-phrases.md` Section 7) |
| A user asks whether their flagged text "is AI" | "This reads as human-written" or "This looks AI-generated" | Neither style nor a score establishes authorship (`detection-principles.md` Section 15) | Explain what the score measures, review the passages for writing problems, assert nothing about origin |
